Finite lattice depth leaves each atom distributed over a finite region of a lattice site.
The resulting phase spread reduces the coherent overlap between the atomic modes used
by the optical and microwave conversion paths. This note first estimates that overlap,
$Q$, and then inserts it into an extension of the transducer model as in
\url{https://github.com/AVKGitHub1/transduction_calcs}.


\section{What the finite-depth QPM factor means}
Let $u_j$ and $v_j$ be the normalized collective coupling profiles of the optical/blue
and UV/microwave paths, including their phases. Define
\begin{equation}
 Q=\left|\sum_j u_j^*v_j\right|,\qquad
 \sum_j|u_j|^2=\sum_j|v_j|^2=1,\qquad 0\leq Q\leq1.
 \label{eq:overlap}
\end{equation}
For matched, uniform coupling envelopes, this becomes
$Q=|\langle e^{i\Delta\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf r}\rangle|$, where, for the chosen conversion
loop, $\Delta\mathbf k=\mathbf k_{780}+\mathbf k_{480}-\mathbf k_{\rm UV}-\mathbf k_\mu$.
Thus $Q$ is an \emph{amplitude overlap}; $Q^2$ is the squared projection onto the useful
collective mode. Neither quantity alone is the converter's success probability.

\subsection{Localization within one site.}
An ideal retroreflected lattice has period $d=\lambda_{\mathrm L}/2$ and reciprocal
wavevector $K=2\kL$, with $\kL=2\pi/\lambda_{\mathrm L}$. For a well of depth $U$,
the deep-lattice harmonic approximation gives~\cite{bloch}
\begin{equation}
 V(x)=U\sin^2(\kL x)\simeq U\kL^2x^2,\quad
 \ER=\frac{\hbar^2\kL^2}{2m},\quad
 \omega_t=\frac{2\sqrt{U\ER}}{\hbar}.
 \label{eq:trap}
\end{equation}
The thermal position variance and the $n$th density Fourier harmonic are then
\begin{align}
 \sigma_x^2&=\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega_t}
       \coth\!\left(\frac{\hbar\omega_t}{2\kb T}\right),\nonumber\\[-1pt]
 b_n&=\left|\left\langle e^{inKx}\right\rangle\right|
     =e^{-(nK)^2\sigma_x^2/2}
     =\exp\!\left[-n^2\sqrt{\frac{\ER}{U}}
           \coth\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{U\ER}}{\kb T}\right)\right].
 \label{eq:bn}
\end{align}
At $T=0$, the hyperbolic cotangent tends to one: zero-point motion still gives
$b_n<1$ for any finite $U$ and nonzero $n$. At fixed temperature, deeper trapping
increases $b_n$ toward one. The approximation requires localization near the well
minimum; appreciable occupation near the barrier calls for actual band wavefunctions.

\subsection{From one axis to the total overlap.}
If $\Delta\mathbf k=\sum_i n_i\mathbf K_i$ exactly and the confined directions are
independent, the factors multiply:
\begin{equation}
 Q=\prod_i b_{n_i,i};\qquad
 Q=b_{2,x}b_{2,y}\quad\hbox{for two second-order QPM axes}.
 \label{eq:qproduct}
\end{equation}
The last expression is the working geometry used below: co-propagating 780 and
480/481 nm fields, perpendicular UV, and suitable lattice axes. It is a
\emph{conditional geometry}, not fixed by the nominal wavelengths alone. A residual
mismatch $\delta\mathbf k$ also produces phase cancellation across occupied site
centres, through $|\langle e^{i\delta\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf R}\rangle|$.
Beam angles, exact wavelengths, and mode envelopes must therefore be checked separately.

\section{Analytical converter model with the orthogonal modes}
\subsection{Problem definition and collective-mode construction.}
This is a derived spatial-mode extension of the supplied converter equations~\cite{model},
in the atomic transduction setting of Ref.~\cite{kumar}. We seek the coherent conversion
amplitude from an optical input to the microwave output when the two atomic coupling
profiles have the overlap $Q$ defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:overlap}. Set the notebook's
$\epsilon=1$ throughout.

\textbf{Atomic variables and assumptions.}
Let $\hat e_j^\dagger$ and $\hat r_j^\dagger$ create an optical and a Rydberg excitation,
respectively, on atom $j$ relative to the prepared reference state. In the weak-excitation
limit these operators are approximately bosonic:
\begin{equation}
 [\hat e_j,\hat e_\ell^\dagger]\simeq
 [\hat r_j,\hat r_\ell^\dagger]\simeq\delta_{j\ell},\qquad
 [\hat e_j,\hat r_\ell^\dagger]\simeq0.
 \label{eq:atomic-bosons}
\end{equation}
Assume frozen atomic positions during conversion, a uniform blue-control magnitude,
and identical atomic detunings and independent decay rates across the ensemble.
UV dressing is already included in the effective microwave coupling and its spatial
profile, as in the calculator. There is no additional UV mode to solve for here.
After absorbing the blue field's spatial phase into $\hat r_j$, its matrix element
$\Om$ can be chosen real and uniform. With the sign convention used below,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\hat H_B}{\hbar}
 =-\Om\sum_j\bigl(\hat e_j^\dagger\hat r_j+
                         \hat r_j^\dagger\hat e_j\bigr).
 \label{eq:uniform-blue}
\end{equation}

\textbf{The two coupling profiles.}
In this phase convention, let $\mathbf u$ describe the collective optical excitation
selected by the optical cavity, and $\mathbf v$ the Rydberg excitation selected by the
UV-dressed microwave interaction. These are the profiles introduced in section~1:
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf u^\dagger\mathbf u=\mathbf v^\dagger\mathbf v=1,\qquad
 \mathcal Q=\mathbf u^\dagger\mathbf v=\sum_j u_j^*v_j,\qquad
 Q=|\mathcal Q|.
 \label{eq:complex-overlap}
\end{equation}
A global phase of $\mathbf v$ can be absorbed into the microwave cavity operator, so
we henceforth choose $\mathbf u^\dagger\mathbf v=Q\geq0$. Writing $s=\sqrt{1-Q^2}$,
the microwave profile has the exact decomposition
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\mathbf v=Q\mathbf u+s\mathbf w,}\qquad
 \mathbf w=\frac{\mathbf v-Q\mathbf u}{s},\qquad
 \mathbf u^\dagger\mathbf w=0,\quad\mathbf w^\dagger\mathbf w=1.
 \label{eq:profile-decomposition}
\end{equation}
The expression for $\mathbf w$ applies when $Q<1$. At $Q=1$, choose any normalized
orthogonal profile: its coupling to the microwave cavity vanishes because $s=0$.

\textbf{Collective operators and closure.}
Define four atomic operators, using uppercase letters to distinguish the operators
from the coherent amplitudes used later:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \hat P^\dagger&=\sum_j u_j\hat e_j^\dagger,&
 \hat R^\dagger&=\sum_j u_j\hat r_j^\dagger,\\
 \hat P_\perp^\dagger&=\sum_j w_j\hat e_j^\dagger,&
 \hat R_\perp^\dagger&=\sum_j w_j\hat r_j^\dagger.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:collective-operators}
\end{equation}
To the same weak-excitation approximation, orthonormality gives unit commutators
within each mode; distinct profiles commute. The optical cavity couples only to
$\hat P$. Uniform blue control couples $\hat P$ to $\hat R$ and $\hat P_\perp$ to
$\hat R_\perp$. The microwave cavity couples to
$Q\hat R+s\hat R_\perp$. The two cavity profiles span at most a two-dimensional
atomic subspace, preserved by the uniform control, detunings, and damping. Hence the
coherently driven response closes on these four atomic modes plus the two cavity modes.
All other orthogonal atomic modes have zero mean drive and are taken initially unexcited.

\textbf{Hamiltonian and conventions.}
Let $\hat a$ and $\hat b$ denote the optical and microwave cavity operators. Retain
$g_o=G/\sqrt2$, $g_m=G_\mu/\sqrt2$, and the blue-control matrix element $\Om$ from
the notebook, where $G$ and $G_\mu$ are the calculator's $G_{\rm opt}$ and $G_{\rm mm}$.
In a rotating frame with zero optical-cavity detuning, the Hamiltonian
restricted to the driven subspace is
\begin{align}
 \frac{\hat H_0}{\hbar}
 &=-\Delta_\mu\hat b^\dagger\hat b
   -\Delta_e(\hat P^\dagger\hat P+\hat P_\perp^\dagger\hat P_\perp)
   -\Delta_r(\hat R^\dagger\hat R+\hat R_\perp^\dagger\hat R_\perp),
   \label{eq:free-hamiltonian}\\
 \frac{\hat H_{\rm int}}{\hbar}
 &=-\Bigl[g_o\hat a^\dagger\hat P
   +\Om(\hat P^\dagger\hat R+\hat P_\perp^\dagger\hat R_\perp)
   +g_m\hat b^\dagger(Q\hat R+s\hat R_\perp)+\mathrm{h.c.}\Bigr].
   \label{eq:interaction-hamiltonian}
\end{align}
The minus signs fix the detuning and coupling-phase convention to match
Eq.~\eqref{eq:six}: use $\dot{\hat c}=(i/\hbar)[\hat H,\hat c]$ and Fourier amplitudes
proportional to $e^{+i\omega t}$. In this convention an optical drive with photon-flux
amplitude $a_{\rm in}(t)$ is represented by
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\hat H_{\rm d}}{\hbar}
 =-\sqrt{\kappa_{o,\mathrm{ex}}}\,
   \bigl[\hat a^\dagger a_{\rm in}(t)+a_{\rm in}^*(t)\hat a\bigr].
 \label{eq:drive-hamiltonian}
\end{equation}
Here $|a_{\rm in}|^2$ is incident photon flux. Define
$a=\langle\hat a\rangle$, $b=\langle\hat b\rangle$,
$p=\langle\hat P\rangle$, $r=\langle\hat R\rangle$,
$p_\perp=\langle\hat P_\perp\rangle$, and
$r_\perp=\langle\hat R_\perp\rangle$.
With no incident microwave field, the useful output has amplitude
$b_{\rm out}=\sqrt{\kappa_{m,\mathrm{ex}}}\,b$ up to an overall port phase, so
$S_{mo}=b_{\rm out}/a_{\rm in}$. Reservoir noise has zero mean in this response
calculation; the transfer efficiency does not characterize added noise.

\subsection{Response functions and linewidth conventions.}
Including damping, the above Fourier and detuning conventions give
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 D_a&=\kappa_o/2+i\omega,&
 D_e&=\Gamma/2+i(\omega-\Delta_e),\\
 D_r&=\gamma_r/2+i(\omega-\Delta_r),&
 D_b&=(\kappa_m+\gamma_r)/2+i(\omega-\Delta_\mu).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:D}
\end{equation}
Here $\kappa_o,\kappa_m$ are total cavity linewidths; $\Gamma,\gamma_r$ set the atomic
coherence damping as shown. The extra $\gamma_r$ in $D_b$ is inherited from the supplied
model, not generated by QPM. The desired port rates are
$\kappa_{o,\mathrm{ex}}$ and $\kappa_{m,\mathrm{ex}}$.

\subsection{Six coupled equations.}
The coherent amplitudes defined above obey, with the chosen input phase,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 D_a a-i g_o p &=i\sqrt{\kappa_{o,\mathrm{ex}}}\,a_{\rm in},\\
 D_e p-i g_o a-i\Om r&=0,\\
 D_r r-i\Om p-i g_m Q b&=0,\\
 D_b b-i g_m\bigl(Qr+\sqrt{1-Q^2}\,r_\perp\bigr)&=0,\\
 D_r r_\perp-i\Om p_\perp-i g_m\sqrt{1-Q^2}\,b&=0,\\
 D_e p_\perp-i\Om r_\perp&=0.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:six}
\end{equation}
The microwave mode couples to $Qr+\sqrt{1-Q^2}\,r_\perp$. Its total collective
coupling remains $g_m$; the useful component is $Qg_m$.

\subsection{Eliminating the orthogonal pair.}
Writing $F=D_eD_r+\Om^2$, the last two equations give
\begin{equation}
 r_\perp=\frac{i g_m\sqrt{1-Q^2}\,D_e}{F}\,b,
 \qquad
 \boxed{\Sigma_\perp(\omega)=\frac{g_m^2(1-Q^2)D_e}{F}}.
 \label{eq:sigma}
\end{equation}
The microwave equation therefore contains $D_b+\Sigma_\perp$. Its real part adds
damping and its imaginary part changes the dispersive response. Simply replacing
$G_\mu$ by $QG_\mu$ drops this response. It must not be added to the useful output-port rate.

\subsection{Closed transfer function.}
Define $Y=D_aF+g_o^2D_r$ and $Z=D_bF+g_m^2D_e$. Solving the remaining equations gives,
up to an overall output phase,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 S_{mo}(\omega)=
 \frac{\sqrt{\kappa_{o,\mathrm{ex}}\kappa_{m,\mathrm{ex}}}\,
       g_og_m\Om QF}
      {YZ+Q^2g_o^2g_m^2\Om^2},\qquad
 \eta(\omega)=|S_{mo}(\omega)|^2 .}
 \label{eq:transfer}
\end{equation}
$Q$ changes both the numerator and the resonant response in the denominator.
This is why the original efficiency cannot generally be scaled by $Q^2$.
At $Q=1$, the orthogonal pair decouples and the original four-mode model is recovered;
at $Q=0$, coherent conversion vanishes.

\section{On-resonance efficiency and the numerical value of \texorpdfstring{$Q$}{Q}}
At $\omega=\Delta_e=\Delta_r=\Delta_\mu=0$, introduce
\begin{equation}
 C_o=\frac{2G^2}{\kappa_o\Gamma},\qquad
 C_m=\frac{2G_\mu^2}{(\kappa_m+\gamma_r)\gamma_r},\qquad
 C_B=\frac{4\Om^2}{\Gamma\gamma_r}.
 \label{eq:coops}
\end{equation}
Let $\eta_o=\kappa_{o,\mathrm{ex}}/\kappa_o$ and
$\eta_m=\kappa_{m,\mathrm{ex}}/\kappa_m$. Equation~\eqref{eq:transfer} becomes
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \eta(0)=\eta_o\eta_m\,\frac{\kappa_m}{\kappa_m+\gamma_r}\,
 \frac{4Q^2C_oC_mC_B(1+C_B)^2}
 {\left[(1+C_B+C_o)(1+C_B+C_m)+Q^2C_oC_mC_B\right]^2}.}
 \label{eq:eta0}
\end{equation}
The port fractions multiply this expression at fixed total linewidths. If a change
in mirror or microwave-port coupling changes a total linewidth, that linewidth and
the cooperativities must also be updated. Additional propagation or detection losses
multiply the port-to-port result when defining an end-to-end efficiency.

\subsection{Lattice example.}
Use $^{85}$Rb, $\lambda_{\mathrm L}=1188\,\mathrm{nm}$, waist $w=200\,\mu\mathrm m$,
100\% retroreflection, and the specified calibration of $3\,\mathrm W$ per beam per
axis to $U/\kb=15\,\mu\mathrm K$. This is an adopted depth calibration, not a new
polarizability calculation. The recoil energy is $\ER/\kb=0.0799004\,\mu\mathrm K$.
For $T=0$ and two equal, exactly matched second-order axes,
\begin{equation}
 b_2=e^{-4\sqrt{\ER/U}}=0.746816,\qquad
 \boxed{Q=b_2^2=0.557734},\qquad Q^2=0.311067.
 \label{eq:qnumber}
\end{equation}
The $T=0$ value is the harmonic lowest-band approximation and includes zero-point motion.
It is a static overlap input to the model; motion-resolved sidebands are not calculated here.

\subsection{Converter example.}
Use the screenshot's parameters, in the calculator's MHz convention
(angular rates divided by $2\pi$):
\begin{equation*}
 \begin{gathered}
 G=15.8474565,\quad G_\mu=2.09170145,\quad \Om=5.52497339,\\
 \kappa_o=10,\quad \kappa_m=5,\quad \Gamma=6,\quad \gamma_r=0.1.
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation*}
All detunings are zero. A common conversion of every rate to angular units leaves
the efficiency unchanged. With $\eta_o=\eta_m=1$:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lc@{}}
\toprule
Model & On-resonance efficiency\\
\midrule
Original converter, $Q=1$ & $82.0957\%$\\
Only $G_\mu\mapsto QG_\mu$; orthogonal pair omitted & $51.5929\%$\\
Six modes with $Q=0.557734$ & $\mathbf{47.0824\%}$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Here $\Sigma_\perp(0)=0.147394\,\mathrm{MHz}$, corresponding to an additional full
linewidth $2\operatorname{Re}\Sigma_\perp=0.294787\,\mathrm{MHz}$ in the same convention.
For example, desired-port fractions of $0.98$ at both cavities give $45.21798\%$ at
unchanged total linewidths, before any further path losses.

\subsection{Scope and checks.}
The closure assumes weak excitation, a frozen spatial distribution, uniform control
magnitude and atomic linewidths, and one effective optical mode profile. Anharmonic
motion, resolved motional sidebands, differential light shifts, and further independent
standing-wave channels require an extended susceptibility model. The closed expression
was checked against the six-equation solution and the $Q=1$ limit in Mathematica.
The efficiencies above are conditional model predictions for the stated geometry.
