Consider an ensemble of $^{85}$Rb atoms coupled to an optical cavity near
$780\,\mathrm{nm}$ and a microwave cavity. A blue control field near
$480/481\,\mathrm{nm}$ connects the optically excited and Rydberg states, while UV
dressing near $297\,\mathrm{nm}$ enables an effective coupling between the prepared
atomic state and the microwave-addressed Rydberg excitation. These interactions form
the conversion loop considered in Ref.~\cite{kumar}. An additional
$1188\,\mathrm{nm}$ lattice confines the atoms and supplies a periodic density pattern
for quasi-phase matching; it is not one of the resonant conversion fields.

Finite lattice depth leaves each atom distributed over a finite region of a lattice
site. The resulting phase spread reduces the coherent overlap, $Q$, between the
atomic modes addressed by the two conversion paths. This note defines that overlap,
derives its effect on the optical-to-microwave response, and evaluates a numerical
example including cavity input and output coupling. All equations and conventions
needed for the derivation are given here. The companion Mathematica notebook
implements and checks them; its notation is mapped to this text at the end~\cite{model}.


\section{What the finite-depth QPM factor means}
\label{sec:qpm}
Label the $N$ atoms by $j=1,\ldots,N$. Let $u_j$ and $v_j$ be the complex excitation
amplitudes on atom $j$ in the normalized collective profiles addressed by the
optical/blue and UV/microwave paths. Each profile includes the relative coupling
magnitudes and optical phases; its overall coupling strength is treated separately
in section~\ref{sec:model}. Define
\begin{equation}
 Q=\left|\sum_j u_j^*v_j\right|,\qquad
 \sum_j|u_j|^2=\sum_j|v_j|^2=1,\qquad 0\leq Q\leq1.
 \label{eq:overlap}
\end{equation}
For matched, uniform coupling envelopes, this becomes
$Q=|\langle e^{i\Delta\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf r}\rangle|$, where the average is over the
normalized atomic spatial distribution. For the chosen conversion loop,
$\Delta\mathbf k=\mathbf k_{780}+\mathbf k_{480}-\mathbf k_{\rm UV}-\mathbf k_\mu$;
each wavevector includes its propagation direction. Reversing the loop changes the
sign of this mismatch but not the magnitude $Q$.
Thus $Q$ is an \emph{amplitude overlap}; $Q^2$ is the squared projection onto the useful
collective mode. Neither quantity alone is the converter's success probability.
The efficiency also depends on coherent exchange, atomic decay, and the cavity ports.

\subsection{Localization within one site.}
An ideal retroreflected lattice has period $d=\lambda_{\mathrm L}/2$ and reciprocal
wavevector $K=2\kL$, with $\kL=2\pi/\lambda_{\mathrm L}$. Let $x$ measure displacement
from a site minimum and $U$ be the energy from that minimum to the barrier. For atomic
mass $m$, the recoil energy $\ER$ and the harmonic trap angular frequency $\omega_t$
are~\cite{bloch}
\begin{equation}
 V(x)=U\sin^2(\kL x)\simeq U\kL^2x^2,\quad
 \ER=\frac{\hbar^2\kL^2}{2m},\quad
 \omega_t=\frac{2\sqrt{U\ER}}{\hbar}.
 \label{eq:trap}
\end{equation}
Here $\hbar$ is the reduced Planck constant. At temperature $T$, with Boltzmann
constant $\kb$, the thermal position variance and the $n$th density Fourier harmonic
are then
\begin{align}
 \sigma_x^2&=\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega_t}
       \coth\!\left(\frac{\hbar\omega_t}{2\kb T}\right),\nonumber\\[-1pt]
 b_n&=\left|\left\langle e^{inKx}\right\rangle\right|
     =e^{-(nK)^2\sigma_x^2/2}
     =\exp\!\left[-n^2\sqrt{\frac{\ER}{U}}
           \coth\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{U\ER}}{\kb T}\right)\right].
 \label{eq:bn}
\end{align}
The integer $n$ labels a spatial Fourier harmonic, not a vibrational band.
At $T=0$, the hyperbolic cotangent tends to one: zero-point motion still gives
$b_n<1$ for any finite $U$ and nonzero $n$. At fixed temperature, deeper trapping
increases $b_n$ toward one. The approximation requires localization near the well
minimum; appreciable occupation near the barrier calls for actual band wavefunctions.

\subsection{From one axis to the total overlap.}
Write an atom's position as its lattice-site centre $\mathbf R$ plus its displacement
within the site. If $\Delta\mathbf k=\sum_i n_i\mathbf K_i$ for integer orders $n_i$,
the phase at every equivalent site centre is the same. This is quasi-phase matching:
the lattice removes cancellation between sites, while finite localization within
each site still reduces the overlap. For independent confined directions, the
remaining factors multiply:
\begin{equation}
 Q=\prod_i b_{n_i,i};\qquad
 Q=b_{2,x}b_{2,y}\quad\hbox{for two second-order QPM axes}.
 \label{eq:qproduct}
\end{equation}
The last expression is the working geometry used below: co-propagating 780 and
480/481 nm fields, perpendicular UV, and suitable lattice axes. It is a
\emph{conditional geometry}, not fixed by the nominal wavelengths alone. A residual
mismatch $\delta\mathbf k$ also produces phase cancellation across occupied site
centres, through $|\langle e^{i\delta\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf R}\rangle|$.
Beam angles, exact wavelengths, and mode envelopes must therefore be checked separately.

\section{Analytical converter model with the orthogonal modes}
\label{sec:model}
\subsection{Problem definition and collective-mode construction.}
The system contains an optical cavity mode $\hat a$, a microwave cavity mode
$\hat b$, and an ensemble with optically excited and Rydberg excitations. The optical
cavity couples to one collective atomic profile with strength $g_o$; the UV-dressed
microwave interaction couples to another with strength $g_m$. The blue field connects
the optical and Rydberg excitations with matrix element $\Om$. The profiles need not
coincide: their normalized overlap is $Q$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:overlap}.

We drive only the optical input and seek the coherent microwave output in the
linear, weak-excitation regime. The conversion efficiency is the ratio of output
microwave photon flux to input optical photon flux. It is not an energy-efficiency
ratio: the control fields account for the difference between the photon energies.
All couplings, detunings, and linewidths in the analytical equations are angular
frequencies; numerical values divided by $2\pi$ are stated explicitly in section~\ref{sec:example}.

\subsubsection{Atomic variables and assumptions.}
Let $\hat e_j^\dagger$ and $\hat r_j^\dagger$ create an optical and a Rydberg excitation,
respectively, on atom $j$ relative to the prepared reference state. The optical
excitation is a ground-to-optically-excited-state coherence; the Rydberg excitation
is the effective coherence addressed by the blue and UV-dressed microwave paths.
When the number of signal excitations is small compared with the number of prepared
atoms, depletion and saturation can be neglected and these operators are approximately
bosonic:
\begin{equation}
 [\hat e_j,\hat e_\ell^\dagger]\simeq
 [\hat r_j,\hat r_\ell^\dagger]\simeq\delta_{j\ell},\qquad
 [\hat e_j,\hat r_\ell^\dagger]\simeq0.
 \label{eq:atomic-bosons}
\end{equation}
Assume frozen atomic positions during conversion, a uniform blue-control magnitude,
and identical atomic detunings and independent decay rates across the ensemble.
UV dressing is already included in the effective microwave coupling and its spatial
profile.
After absorbing the blue field's spatial phase into $\hat r_j$, its matrix element
$\Om$ can be chosen real and uniform. It is defined as the Hamiltonian matrix
element below, so a Rabi-frequency convention that puts a factor of $1/2$ in the
Hamiltonian must be converted before substituting a value for $\Om$.
With the sign convention used below,
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\hat H_B}{\hbar}
 =-\Om\sum_j\bigl(\hat e_j^\dagger\hat r_j+
                         \hat r_j^\dagger\hat e_j\bigr).
 \label{eq:uniform-blue}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{The two coupling profiles.}
In this phase convention, let $\mathbf u$ describe the collective optical excitation
selected by the optical cavity, and $\mathbf v$ the Rydberg excitation selected by the
UV-dressed microwave interaction. These are the profiles introduced in section~\ref{sec:qpm}:
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf u^\dagger\mathbf u=\mathbf v^\dagger\mathbf v=1,\qquad
 \mathcal Q=\mathbf u^\dagger\mathbf v=\sum_j u_j^*v_j,\qquad
 Q=|\mathcal Q|.
 \label{eq:complex-overlap}
\end{equation}
A global phase of $\mathbf v$ can be absorbed into the microwave cavity operator, so
we henceforth choose $\mathbf u^\dagger\mathbf v=Q\geq0$. Writing $s=\sqrt{1-Q^2}$,
the microwave profile has the exact decomposition
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{\mathbf v=Q\mathbf u+s\mathbf w,}\qquad
 \mathbf w=\frac{\mathbf v-Q\mathbf u}{s},\qquad
 \mathbf u^\dagger\mathbf w=0,\quad\mathbf w^\dagger\mathbf w=1.
 \label{eq:profile-decomposition}
\end{equation}
The expression for $\mathbf w$ applies when $Q<1$. At $Q=1$, choose any normalized
orthogonal profile: its coupling to the microwave cavity vanishes because $s=0$.

\subsubsection{Collective operators and closure.}
Define four atomic operators, using uppercase letters to distinguish the operators
from the coherent amplitudes used later:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \hat P^\dagger&=\sum_j u_j\hat e_j^\dagger,&
 \hat R^\dagger&=\sum_j u_j\hat r_j^\dagger,\\
 \hat P_\perp^\dagger&=\sum_j w_j\hat e_j^\dagger,&
 \hat R_\perp^\dagger&=\sum_j w_j\hat r_j^\dagger.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:collective-operators}
\end{equation}
To the same weak-excitation approximation, orthonormality gives unit commutators
within each mode; distinct profiles commute. The corresponding coupling chain is
\begin{equation}
 a
 \xleftrightarrow{g_o} E_1
 \xleftrightarrow{\Omega_B} R_1
 \xleftrightarrow{Qg_m} b
 \xleftrightarrow{s g_m} R_2
 \xleftrightarrow{\Omega_B} E_2 .
 \label{eq:coupling-chain}
\end{equation}
Here $a,b$ label the optical and microwave cavity modes. The chain labels
$E_1,R_1,R_2,E_2$ denote the modes represented by
$\hat P,\hat R,\hat R_\perp,\hat P_\perp$, respectively, and
$\Omega_B=\Om$; $s=\sqrt{1-Q^2}$ as above.
The two cavity profiles span at most a two-dimensional
atomic subspace, preserved by the uniform control, detunings, and damping. Hence the
coherently driven response closes on these four atomic modes plus the two cavity modes.
All other orthogonal atomic modes have zero mean drive and are taken initially unexcited.

\subsubsection{Hamiltonian and conventions.}
The strengths $g_o$ and $g_m$ are collective Hamiltonian matrix elements, including
the total coupling magnitude of each normalized profile. They are not single-atom
couplings and are held fixed when varying $Q$. To use the parameter convention of
the baseline converter model~\cite{model}, define
\begin{equation}
 g_o=\frac{G}{\sqrt2},\qquad g_m=\frac{G_\mu}{\sqrt2}.
 \label{eq:coupling-convention}
\end{equation}
Here $G$ and $G_\mu$ are alternative names for the collective coupling scales, not
additional interactions. The parameters $\Delta_e$, $\Delta_r$, and $\Delta_\mu$
describe the optical atomic, effective Rydberg, and microwave cavity detunings in
the rotating frame. Their signs are fixed by the following free Hamiltonian, with
zero optical-cavity detuning. Restricted to the driven subspace,
\begin{align}
 \frac{\hat H_0}{\hbar}
 &=-\Delta_\mu\hat b^\dagger\hat b
   -\Delta_e(\hat P^\dagger\hat P+\hat P_\perp^\dagger\hat P_\perp)
   -\Delta_r(\hat R^\dagger\hat R+\hat R_\perp^\dagger\hat R_\perp),
   \label{eq:free-hamiltonian}\\
 \frac{\hat H_{\rm int}}{\hbar}
 &=-\Bigl[g_o\hat a^\dagger\hat P
   +\Om(\hat P^\dagger\hat R+\hat P_\perp^\dagger\hat R_\perp)
   +g_m\hat b^\dagger(Q\hat R+s\hat R_\perp)+\mathrm{h.c.}\Bigr].
   \label{eq:interaction-hamiltonian}
\end{align}
The minus signs fix the detuning and coupling-phase convention to match
Eq.~\eqref{eq:six}: use $\dot{\hat c}=(i/\hbar)[\hat H,\hat c]$ and Fourier amplitudes
proportional to $e^{+i\omega t}$, where $\omega$ is the spectral offset from the
chosen reference. Let $\kappa_{o,\mathrm{ex}}$ and $\kappa_{m,\mathrm{ex}}$ be the
optical input-port and useful microwave output-port coupling rates, respectively.
In this convention an optical drive with photon-flux amplitude $a_{\rm in}(t)$ is
represented by
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\hat H_{\rm d}}{\hbar}
 =-\sqrt{\kappa_{o,\mathrm{ex}}}\,
   \bigl[\hat a^\dagger a_{\rm in}(t)+a_{\rm in}^*(t)\hat a\bigr].
 \label{eq:drive-hamiltonian}
\end{equation}
Here $|a_{\rm in}|^2$ is incident photon flux. Define
$a=\langle\hat a\rangle$, $b=\langle\hat b\rangle$,
$p=\langle\hat P\rangle$, $r=\langle\hat R\rangle$,
$p_\perp=\langle\hat P_\perp\rangle$, and
$r_\perp=\langle\hat R_\perp\rangle$.
With no incident microwave field, the useful output has amplitude
$b_{\rm out}=\sqrt{\kappa_{m,\mathrm{ex}}}\,b$ up to an overall port phase, so
$S_{mo}=b_{\rm out}/a_{\rm in}$. Reservoir noise has zero mean in this response
calculation; the transfer efficiency does not characterize added noise.

\subsection{Response functions and linewidth conventions.}
Let $\kappa_o$ and $\kappa_m$ be the total cavity energy decay rates, including all
cavity ports and internal loss. Thus $\kappa_{o,\mathrm{ex}}\leq\kappa_o$ and
$\kappa_{m,\mathrm{ex}}\leq\kappa_m$. Define the atomic linewidth parameters
$\Gamma$ and $\gamma_r$ so that the optical and Rydberg amplitudes decay at
$\Gamma/2$ and $\gamma_r/2$, respectively.

To preserve the baseline converter equations~\cite{model}, this model also assigns
an additional amplitude damping $\gamma_r/2$ to the microwave mode: its effective
full width is $\kappa_m+\gamma_r$. This is a separate phenomenological assumption
of the baseline model, not a consequence of QPM or a change to the useful port rate.
Including these damping terms, the inverse response functions are
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 D_a&=\kappa_o/2+i\omega,&
 D_e&=\Gamma/2+i(\omega-\Delta_e),\\
 D_r&=\gamma_r/2+i(\omega-\Delta_r),&
 D_b&=(\kappa_m+\gamma_r)/2+i(\omega-\Delta_\mu).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:D}
\end{equation}
The real parts set amplitude damping and the imaginary parts describe detuning.
The additional response due to the orthogonal atomic modes will be derived separately;
it is not included in these definitions.

\subsection{Six coupled equations.}
The coherent amplitudes defined above obey, with the chosen input phase,
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 D_a a-i g_o p &=i\sqrt{\kappa_{o,\mathrm{ex}}}\,a_{\rm in},\\
 D_e p-i g_o a-i\Om r&=0,\\
 D_r r-i\Om p-i g_m Q b&=0,\\
 D_b b-i g_m\bigl(Qr+\sqrt{1-Q^2}\,r_\perp\bigr)&=0,\\
 D_r r_\perp-i\Om p_\perp-i g_m\sqrt{1-Q^2}\,b&=0,\\
 D_e p_\perp-i\Om r_\perp&=0.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:six}
\end{equation}
The microwave mode couples to $Qr+\sqrt{1-Q^2}\,r_\perp$. Its total collective
coupling remains $g_m$; the useful component is $Qg_m$.

\subsection{Eliminating the orthogonal pair.}
The last equation gives $p_\perp=i\Om r_\perp/D_e$. Substituting this into the
$r_\perp$ equation and writing $F=D_eD_r+\Om^2$ gives
\begin{equation}
 r_\perp=\frac{i g_m\sqrt{1-Q^2}\,D_e}{F}\,b,
 \qquad
 \boxed{\Sigma_\perp(\omega)=\frac{g_m^2(1-Q^2)D_e}{F}}.
 \label{eq:sigma}
\end{equation}
The microwave equation therefore contains $D_b+\Sigma_\perp$.
The quantity $\Sigma_\perp$ is the response of the eliminated pair: its real part
adds amplitude damping and its imaginary part changes the dispersive response.
The associated additional full linewidth is $2\operatorname{Re}\Sigma_\perp$.
Although this pair has no direct optical-cavity coupling, the microwave field drives
it; it feeds back on that field and can dissipate through atomic decay. Simply
replacing $G_\mu$ by $QG_\mu$ retains the useful projection but drops this feedback.
Neither $\Sigma_\perp$ nor the baseline extra damping belongs in the useful output-port rate.

\subsection{Closed transfer function.}
After eliminating the orthogonal pair, put $B=D_b+\Sigma_\perp$. Eliminating the
remaining atomic amplitudes gives
\begin{equation}
 S_{mo}=\frac{\sqrt{\kappa_{o,\mathrm{ex}}\kappa_{m,\mathrm{ex}}}\,
                    g_og_m\Om Q}
 {B(D_aF+g_o^2D_r)+g_m^2Q^2(D_aD_e+g_o^2)}.
 \label{eq:transfer-eliminated}
\end{equation}
Define $Y=D_aF+g_o^2D_r$ and $Z=D_bF+g_m^2D_e$. Substituting for $\Sigma_\perp$
and using $F=D_eD_r+\Om^2$ then gives the compact result, up to an overall output phase,
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 S_{mo}(\omega)=
 \frac{\sqrt{\kappa_{o,\mathrm{ex}}\kappa_{m,\mathrm{ex}}}\,
       g_og_m\Om QF}
      {YZ+Q^2g_o^2g_m^2\Om^2},\qquad
 \eta(\omega)=|S_{mo}(\omega)|^2 .}
 \label{eq:transfer}
\end{equation}
$Q$ changes both the numerator and the resonant response in the denominator.
This is why the matched-profile efficiency cannot generally be scaled by $Q^2$.
At $Q=1$, the orthogonal pair decouples and the four-mode baseline model is recovered;
at $Q=0$, coherent conversion vanishes.

\section{On-resonance efficiency and the numerical value of \texorpdfstring{$Q$}{Q}}
\label{sec:example}
At $\omega=\Delta_e=\Delta_r=\Delta_\mu=0$, introduce the dimensionless
cooperativities, which compare coherent coupling strengths with damping:
\begin{equation}
 C_o=\frac{2G^2}{\kappa_o\Gamma},\qquad
 C_m=\frac{2G_\mu^2}{(\kappa_m+\gamma_r)\gamma_r},\qquad
 C_B=\frac{4\Om^2}{\Gamma\gamma_r}.
 \label{eq:coops}
\end{equation}
Let $\eta_o=\kappa_{o,\mathrm{ex}}/\kappa_o$ and
$\eta_m=\kappa_{m,\mathrm{ex}}/\kappa_m$. Equation~\eqref{eq:transfer} becomes
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \eta(0)=\eta_o\eta_m\,\frac{\kappa_m}{\kappa_m+\gamma_r}\,
 \frac{4Q^2C_oC_mC_B(1+C_B)^2}
 {\left[(1+C_B+C_o)(1+C_B+C_m)+Q^2C_oC_mC_B\right]^2}.}
 \label{eq:eta0}
\end{equation}
The port fractions multiply this expression at fixed total linewidths. If a change
in mirror or microwave-port coupling changes a total linewidth, that linewidth and
the cooperativities must also be updated. Additional propagation or detection losses
multiply the port-to-port result when defining an end-to-end efficiency.

\subsection{Lattice example.}
Use $^{85}$Rb, $\lambda_{\mathrm L}=1188\,\mathrm{nm}$, waist $w=200\,\mu\mathrm m$,
100\% retroreflection, and the specified calibration of $3\,\mathrm W$ per beam per
axis to $U/\kb=15\,\mu\mathrm K$. This is an adopted depth calibration, not a new
polarizability calculation. The recoil energy is $\ER/\kb=0.0799004\,\mu\mathrm K$.
For $T=0$ and two equal, exactly matched second-order axes,
\begin{equation}
 b_2=e^{-4\sqrt{\ER/U}}=0.746816,\qquad
 \boxed{Q=b_2^2=0.557734},\qquad Q^2=0.311067.
 \label{eq:qnumber}
\end{equation}
The $T=0$ value is the harmonic lowest-band approximation and includes zero-point motion.
It is a static overlap input to the model; motion-resolved sidebands are not calculated here.

\subsection{Converter example.}
For a reproducible reference case, take the following collective coupling and
linewidth parameters:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \frac{(G,G_\mu,\Om)}{2\pi}
   &=(15.8474565,\,2.09170145,\,5.52497339)\,\mathrm{MHz},\\
 \frac{(\kappa_o,\kappa_m,\Gamma,\gamma_r)}{2\pi}
   &=(10,\,5,\,6,\,0.1)\,\mathrm{MHz}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:benchmark}
\end{equation}
All detunings are zero. Since the efficiency is dimensionless, one may evaluate it
using the listed MHz numbers if every rate and frequency is divided by the same
$2\pi$ factor. With $\eta_o=\eta_m=1$, and $Q$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:qnumber} for
the two finite-depth cases:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lc@{}}
\toprule
Model & On-resonance efficiency\\
\midrule
Matched profiles, $Q=1$ & $82.0957\%$\\
Only $G_\mu\mapsto QG_\mu$; orthogonal pair omitted & $51.5929\%$\\
Six modes with $Q=0.557734$ & $\mathbf{47.0824\%}$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Here $\Sigma_\perp(0)/(2\pi)=0.147394\,\mathrm{MHz}$, corresponding to an additional
full linewidth $2\operatorname{Re}\Sigma_\perp(0)/(2\pi)=0.294787\,\mathrm{MHz}$.
For example, desired-port fractions of $0.98$ at both cavities give $45.21798\%$ at
unchanged total linewidths, before any further path losses.

\subsection{Scope and checks.}
The closure assumes weak excitation, a frozen spatial distribution, uniform control
magnitude and atomic linewidths, and one effective optical mode profile. Anharmonic
motion, resolved motional sidebands, differential light shifts, and further independent
standing-wave channels require an extended susceptibility model. The closed expression
was checked against the six-equation solution and the $Q=1$ limit in Mathematica.
The efficiencies above are conditional model predictions for the stated geometry.

\subsection{Using the companion Mathematica notebook.}
The companion \texttt{Abhishek\_Converter\_QPM.nb} follows the same progression:
solve the six equations, eliminate the orthogonal pair, check the $Q=1$ limit, and
substitute numerical parameters. Its optical linewidth $\kappa$ is $\kappa_o$ here,
and its microwave linewidth $\kappa_\mu$ is $\kappa_m$. The symbols $G$, $G_\mu$,
$\Om$, $\Gamma$, $\gamma_r$, and the detunings retain their definitions above.
The calculator names $G_{\rm opt}$ and $G_{\rm mm}$ correspond to $G$ and $G_\mu$.
Set the notebook's auxiliary parameter $\epsilon=1$ when comparing with this note.

The notebook retains a forcing parameter \texttt{Ein} in its first equation and
defines \texttt{ConverterEfficQ} as $\sqrt{\kappa_o\kappa_m}\,b$ in that normalization.
It is therefore an amplitude, despite its name. The connection to the physical,
input-normalized response is
\begin{equation}
 S_{mo}=\sqrt{\eta_o\eta_m}\,
       \frac{\texttt{ConverterEfficQ}}{\texttt{Ein}},\qquad
 \eta=\eta_o\eta_m\,\texttt{NormalizedEfficiencyQ},
 \label{eq:notebook-normalization}
\end{equation}
up to the same overall port phase, with $\epsilon=1$.
Here \texttt{NormalizedEfficiencyQ} is
$\texttt{EfficiencyQ}/\texttt{Ein}^{2}$ for the real input convention in the notebook.
The numerical examples use \texttt{Ein}=1; a different drive amplitude must not
change the normalized efficiency in this linear model.

The stored expression \texttt{SigmaPerp} is Eq.~\eqref{eq:sigma}, and
\texttt{OnResonanceEfficiencyQ} is Eq.~\eqref{eq:eta0} with unit port fractions.
The preset \texttt{parmsScreenshot} contains the reference parameters of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:benchmark}; \texttt{parmsOriginal} is a different parameter set from
the original notebook. Keep $Q$ symbolic until applying \texttt{qRule} in the
numerical example. The notebook calls its two matched axes $x,z$ instead of $x,y$;
these are labels for the same two-factor overlap assumption, not an extra axis.
Finally, \texttt{etaOpt} and \texttt{etaMM} are the port fractions $\eta_o$ and
$\eta_m$, applied through \texttt{EfficiencyQWithPorts} at fixed total linewidths.
