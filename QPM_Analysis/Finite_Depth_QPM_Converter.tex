\title{Finite-depth Quasi-Phase Matching (QPM) and Converter Efficiency}
\author{Abhishek V. Karve}
\affiliation{Department of Applied Physics, Stanford University}
\date{\today}
\maketitle

An ensemble of $^{85}$Rb atoms couples optical (780 nm) and microwave cavities
via an \textbf{assumed uniform} blue (480/481 nm) control field and UV (297 nm) dressing~\cite{kumar}.
Optical-to-microwave conversion absorbs optical and blue photons and emits
microwave and UV photons; reversing the loop reverses conversion.
A separate 1188 nm lattice creates periodic density for quasi-phase
matching (QPM). Finite site width reduces the overlap between the collective
atomic profiles. We derive its effect on coherent photon-number conversion
efficiency, accounting for atomic loss, reflection, and port coupling.

\section{Spatial overlap and lattice localization}
\label{sec:qpm}
Let $\mathbf u,\mathbf v$ be normalized optical/blue and UV/microwave excitation
profiles, including relative coupling magnitudes and phases. Their amplitude overlap is
\begin{equation}
 Q=|\mathbf u^\dagger\mathbf v|,\qquad
 \mathbf u^\dagger\mathbf u=\mathbf v^\dagger\mathbf v=1,\qquad 0\leq Q\leq1.
 \label{eq:overlap}
\end{equation}
For traveling-wave fields with matched uniform envelopes,
$Q=|\langle e^{i\Delta\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf r}\rangle|$, averaged over the atomic
density, with
$\Delta\mathbf k=\mathbf k_{780}+\mathbf k_{\rm blue}-\mathbf k_{\rm UV}-\mathbf k_\mu$.
Wavevectors include propagation directions. Thus $Q^2$ gives the projection onto
the useful mode; efficiency also depends on the coupled dynamics and losses.

For lattice period $d=\lambda_{\mathrm L}/2$, define $\kL=2\pi/\lambda_{\mathrm L}$
and $K=2\kL$. For lattice depth $U$, the harmonic approximation near a minimum gives~\cite{bloch}
\begin{equation}
 V(x)=U\sin^2(\kL x)\simeq U\kL^2x^2,\qquad
 \ER=\frac{\hbar^2\kL^2}{2m},\qquad
 \omega_t=\frac{2\sqrt{U\ER}}{\hbar}.
 \label{eq:trap}
\end{equation}
At temperature $T$, the position variance and density harmonics are
\begin{align}
 \sigma_x^2&=\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega_t}
       \coth\!\left(\frac{\hbar\omega_t}{2\kb T}\right),\nonumber\\
 b_n&=|\langle e^{inKx}\rangle|
 =e^{-(nK)^2\sigma_x^2/2}
 =\exp\!\left[-n^2\sqrt{\frac{\ER}{U}}
       \coth\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{U\ER}}{\kb T}\right)\right].
 \label{eq:bn}
\end{align}
Here $n$ labels the spatial harmonic. Even at $T=0$, zero-point motion gives
$b_n<1$ for $n\ne0$; deeper trapping increases the overlap. The harmonic approximation
requires localization near a minimum.

Exact matching, $\Delta\mathbf k=\sum_i n_i\mathbf K_i$, aligns the phases of
occupied site centres. Independent displacements within sites then give
\begin{equation}
 Q=\prod_i b_{n_i,i};\qquad Q=b_{2,x}\; b_{2,y}
 \quad\text{for two second-order axes}.
 \label{eq:qproduct}
\end{equation}
For example, take optical and blue beams along $+x$, UV along $+y$, and
$\mathbf K_i=K\hat{\mathbf i}$. The target $(n_x,n_y)=(2,-2)$ requires
\begin{equation}
 k_{780}+k_{\rm blue}-k_{\mu,x}=2K,\qquad
 -k_{\rm UV}-k_{\mu,y}=-2K,\qquad k_{\mu,z}=0.
 \label{eq:geometry-components}
\end{equation}
Exact matching depends on the wavelengths and beam angles.
Neglecting $\mathbf k_\mu$, nominal wavelengths of 780, 1188, and 480 (481) nm give
$\delta k_x=-0.01017$ ($-0.03738$) rad/$\mu$m. For Gaussian site-centre rms widths
$L_i$, residual mismatch adds the factor
$\exp[-\sum_i(\delta k_iL_i)^2/2]$; negligible suppression requires
$|\delta k_i|L_i\ll1$. Site factors and mode envelopes must also be evaluated
for the actual geometry. The benchmark below assumes exact matching.

\section{Six-mode converter response}
\label{sec:model}
\subsection{Collective modes and assumptions}
Let $\hat a,\hat b$ annihilate optical and microwave cavity photons, and let
$\hat e_j^\dagger,\hat r_j^\dagger$ create optical and Rydberg excitations from the
prepared UV-dressed state on atom $j$. UV dressing mixes the ground and auxiliary
Rydberg states; the couplings and profiles include this dressing.
Weak excitation makes these operators approximately bosonic. Assume frozen
positions, uniform blue-control magnitude, identical atomic detunings, and
uniform independent amplitude damping. Absorb the blue phase into $\hat r_j$ and the global
profile phase into $\hat b$, so $\Om$ is real and $\mathbf u^\dagger\mathbf v=Q\geq0$.

With $s=\sqrt{1-Q^2}$, decompose the microwave profile as
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf v=Q\mathbf u+s\mathbf w,\qquad
 \mathbf w=\frac{\mathbf v-Q\mathbf u}{s},\qquad
 \mathbf u^\dagger\mathbf w=0,\quad\mathbf w^\dagger\mathbf w=1.
 \label{eq:profile-decomposition}
\end{equation}
At $Q=1$, choose any orthogonal $\mathbf w$; its microwave coupling vanishes.
The four collective operators are
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \hat P^\dagger&=\sum_j u_j\hat e_j^\dagger,&
 \hat R^\dagger&=\sum_j u_j\hat r_j^\dagger,\\
 \hat P_\perp^\dagger&=\sum_j w_j\hat e_j^\dagger,&
 \hat R_\perp^\dagger&=\sum_j w_j\hat r_j^\dagger.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:collective-operators}
\end{equation}
Uniform control, detunings, and damping preserve this two-profile subspace, so
only these four atomic modes and two cavities are driven. Their coupling chain is
\begin{equation}
 a\xleftrightarrow{g_o}P\xleftrightarrow{\Om}R
 \xleftrightarrow{Qg_m}b\xleftrightarrow{s g_m}R_\perp
 \xleftrightarrow{\Om}P_\perp.
 \label{eq:coupling-chain}
\end{equation}
The orthogonal pair is a distinct excitation pattern of the same atoms. It is
dark to the optical cavity but couples to the microwave cavity and blue field;
atomic decay makes its response dissipative.

The interaction Hamiltonian fixes the coupling convention:
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\hat H_{\rm int}}{\hbar}
 =-\Bigl[g_o\hat a^\dagger\hat P
 +\Om(\hat P^\dagger\hat R+\hat P_\perp^\dagger\hat R_\perp)
 +g_m\hat b^\dagger(Q\hat R+s\hat R_\perp)+\mathrm{h.c.}\Bigr].
 \label{eq:interaction-hamiltonian}
\end{equation}
The collective matrix elements $g_o,g_m$ are held fixed as $Q$ varies.
The notebooks use $g_o=G/\sqrt2$, $g_m=G_\mu/\sqrt2$~\cite{model}; these factors
reflect conventions, not finite-depth corrections. Here $\Om$ is the
Hamiltonian matrix element; Rabi frequencies defined with an extra $1/2$ require conversion.
All rates, detunings, and frequencies are angular frequencies.

\subsection{Damping and driven equations}
Let $\kappa_o,\kappa_m$ be bare cavity energy decay rates, including ports and
internal loss; useful port rates obey $\kappa_{j,\mathrm{ex}}\leq\kappa_j$.
Atomic amplitude decay rates are $\Gamma/2,\gamma_r/2$.
Additional microwave energy decay $\gadd$ includes loss from the lower Rydberg state.

Using Fourier amplitudes $e^{+i\omega t}$ and zero optical-cavity detuning, define
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 D_a&=\kappa_o/2+i\omega,&
 D_e&=\Gamma/2+i(\omega-\Delta_e),\\
 D_r&=\gamma_r/2+i(\omega-\Delta_r),&
 D_b&=(\kappa_m+\gadd)/2+i(\omega-\Delta_\mu).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:D}
\end{equation}
Lowercase variables denote the corresponding mean amplitudes.
For optical input $a_{\rm in}$ with photon flux $|a_{\rm in}|^2$, the response is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 D_a a-i g_o p &=i\sqrt{\kappa_{o,\mathrm{ex}}}\,a_{\rm in},\\
 D_e p-i g_o a-i\Om r&=0,\\
 D_r r-i\Om p-i g_m Qb&=0,\\
 D_b b-i g_m(Qr+s r_\perp)&=0,\\
 D_r r_\perp-i\Om p_\perp-i g_m s b&=0,\\
 D_e p_\perp-i\Om r_\perp&=0.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:six}
\end{equation}
With no microwave input, $b_{\rm out}=\sqrt{\kappa_{m,\mathrm{ex}}}\,b$ up to a
port phase. The transfer amplitude $S_{mo}=b_{\rm out}/a_{\rm in}$ gives the coherent
photon-number efficiency $\eta=|S_{mo}|^2$, not energy efficiency or added noise.

\subsection{Atomic feedback and transfer function}
Set $F=D_eD_r+\Om^2$. Eliminating the orthogonal pair gives
\begin{equation}
 r_\perp=\frac{i g_m s D_e}{F}\,b,\qquad
 \Sigma_\perp=\frac{g_m^2(1-Q^2)D_e}{F}.
 \label{eq:sigma}
\end{equation}
This changes $D_b$ to $D_b+\Sigma_\perp$, with frequency-dependent energy-decay
contribution $2\operatorname{Re}\Sigma_\perp$ and dispersion $\operatorname{Im}\Sigma_\perp$.
This loading does not contribute to the useful output-port rate.

Eliminating all atomic amplitudes gives the exact frequency-domain response
\begin{equation}
 \begin{pmatrix}A&iJ\\iJ&B\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}a\\b\end{pmatrix}
 =\begin{pmatrix}i\sqrt{\kappa_{o,\mathrm{ex}}}\,a_{\rm in}\\0\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 A=D_a+\frac{g_o^2D_r}{F},\quad
 B=D_b+\frac{g_m^2D_e}{F},\quad J=\frac{Qg_og_m\Om}{F},
 \label{eq:effective-cavities}
\end{equation}
without assuming fast atomic relaxation. The transfer function is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{S_{mo}(\omega)=
 \frac{\sqrt{\kappa_{o,\mathrm{ex}}\kappa_{m,\mathrm{ex}}}\,J}{AB+J^2},
 \qquad \eta(\omega)=|S_{mo}(\omega)|^2.}
 \label{eq:transfer}
\end{equation}
At fixed couplings and linewidths, $A,B$ are independent of $Q$, whereas $J\propto Q$:
the useful and orthogonal contributions to microwave loading sum as $Q^2+(1-Q^2)=1$.
Replacing $g_m$ by $Qg_m$ incorrectly reduces the total loading as well as the
cross coupling. At $Q=1$ the orthogonal pair decouples; at $Q=0$ conversion vanishes.

\subsection{On-resonance efficiency}
For comparison with the notebooks, define
\begin{equation}
 C_o=\frac{2G^2}{\kappa_o\Gamma},\qquad
 C_m=\frac{2G_\mu^2}{(\kappa_m+\gadd)\gamma_r},\qquad
 C_B=\frac{4\Om^2}{\Gamma\gamma_r},\qquad
 \eta_j=\frac{\kappa_{j,\mathrm{ex}}}{\kappa_j}.
 \label{eq:coops}
\end{equation}
At $\omega=\Delta_e=\Delta_r=\Delta_\mu=0$, Eq.~\eqref{eq:transfer} gives
\begin{equation}
 \eta(0)=\eta_o\eta_m\frac{\kappa_m}{\kappa_m+\gadd}
 \frac{4Q^2C_oC_mC_B(1+C_B)^2}
 {[(1+C_B+C_o)(1+C_B+C_m)+Q^2C_oC_mC_B]^2}.
 \label{eq:eta0}
\end{equation}
The non-QPM model in \texttt{Abhishek\_Converter.nb} assumes fully overlapping
collective profiles. Finite overlap modifies both the numerator and denominator
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:eta0}; setting $Q=1$ recovers the non-QPM efficiency for the same
couplings, losses, and port fractions.

Port fractions multiply efficiency at fixed total linewidths; changing port
coupling requires updated cooperativities. Propagation and detection losses further
reduce this port-to-port efficiency.

\section{Numerical benchmark and loss budget}
\label{sec:example}
\subsection{Lattice and motional scales}
Take $^{85}$Rb, $\lambda_{\mathrm L}=1188$ nm, waist $w=200\,\mu$m, and full
retroreflection. Adopt the calibration $3$ W per beam per axis
$\leftrightarrow U/\kb=15\,\mu$K, without a polarizability calculation.
For $\ER/\kb=0.0799004\,\mu$K, $T=0$, and two exactly matched second-order axes,
\begin{equation}
 b_2=e^{-4\sqrt{\ER/U}}=0.746816,\qquad
 Q=b_2^2=0.557734,\qquad Q^2=0.311067.
 \label{eq:qnumber}
\end{equation}
The lattice has $U/\ER=187.7$, $\sigma_x=36.12$ nm, and $2K\sigma_x=0.7641$.
Thus a deep lattice still leaves substantial phase spread at the second harmonic;
the overlap reductions multiply across the two axes. The trap frequency is
$\omega_t/(2\pi)=45.62$ kHz, with $\hbar\omega_t/\kb=2.190\,\mu$K.
The ground-state benchmark requires a temperature well below this scale;
at $T=1\,\mu$K the same harmonic formula gives $Q=0.48137$.

Treating this density as static requires unresolved motional sidebands and
negligible Doppler and recoil phases during $\tau_{\rm resp}$. The Gaussian average
retains the full phase spread $nK\sigma_x$; the calculation describes the coherent
response, not the inelastic motional spectrum.

\subsection{Conversion efficiency}
Use zero detunings, $\gadd=\gamma_r$, and
\begin{equation}
 \frac{(G,G_\mu,\Om)}{2\pi}=(15.8474565,\,2.09170145,\,5.52497339)\,\mathrm{MHz},
 \qquad
 \frac{(\kappa_o,\kappa_m,\Gamma,\gamma_r)}{2\pi}=(10,\,5,\,6,\,0.1)\,\mathrm{MHz}.
 \label{eq:benchmark}
\end{equation}
Dividing every rate and frequency by $2\pi$ leaves efficiency unchanged.
Figure~\ref{fig:comparison} compares the unit-port spectra. The peak efficiency
falls from $82.0957\%$ at $Q=1$ to $47.0824\%$ at $T=0$ and $38.15\%$ at
$T=1\,\mu$K. Naive $Q^2$ scaling instead gives $25.5373\%$ at $T=0$.

\begin{figure}[ht]
 \centering
 \includegraphics[width=0.6\textwidth]{figs/Comparison_plot.png}
 \caption{Conversion spectra from Eq.~\eqref{eq:transfer} versus $\omega/(2\pi)$, with parameters in Eq.~\eqref{eq:benchmark},
 unit port fractions, and $U/\kb=15\,\mu$K per axis. QPM overlaps follow Eqs.~\eqref{eq:bn}--\eqref{eq:qproduct}.}
 \label{fig:comparison}
\end{figure}

At $T=0$ and resonance, $\Sigma_\perp/(2\pi)=0.147394$ MHz gives an energy-decay
contribution $2\operatorname{Re}\Sigma_\perp/(2\pi)=0.294787$ MHz.
The dressed responses are $A/(2\pi)=5.20468$ MHz and
$B/(2\pi)=2.76394$ MHz. At $T=0$, changing only the port fractions to
$\eta_o=\eta_m=0.98$ gives $45.2180\%$; setting only $\gadd=0$ gives $48.54\%$
at unit ports.

The slowest amplitude decay rate of the six-mode drift matrix is
$2\pi\times1.4533$ MHz, so $\tau_{\rm resp}=0.1095\,\mu$s and
$\omega_t\tau_{\rm resp}=0.0314$. Combined with the site width, this supports frozen
motion; a narrower response requires rechecking these conditions.

\subsection{Reflection and dissipation}
With the drive convention of Eq.~\eqref{eq:six},
$a_{\rm out}=a_{\rm in}+i\sqrt{\kappa_{o,\mathrm{ex}}}\,a$.
For incident flux $\Phi=|a_{\rm in}|^2$, define
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal R_o&=\left|1+i\sqrt{\kappa_{o,\mathrm{ex}}}\,a/a_{\rm in}\right|^2,&
 \mathcal L_{\rm at}&=\frac{\Gamma(|p|^2+|p_\perp|^2)+\gamma_r(|r|^2+|r_\perp|^2)}{\Phi},\\
 \mathcal L_o&=\frac{(\kappa_o-\kappa_{o,\mathrm{ex}})|a|^2}{\Phi},&
 \mathcal L_m&=\frac{(\kappa_m-\kappa_{m,\mathrm{ex}}+\gadd)|b|^2}{\Phi}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:flux-budget}
\end{equation}
The passive model obeys $\mathcal R_o+\eta+\mathcal L_{\rm at}+\mathcal L_o+\mathcal L_m=1$.
On resonance, the unit-port flux budget (percent) is
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}l@{\hspace{2em}}r@{\hspace{1.5em}}r@{}}
\toprule
Channel & $Q=1$ & $Q=0.557734$\\
\midrule
Microwave output & 82.0957 & 47.0824\\
Optical reflection & 3.4748 & 37.3236\\
Total atomic dissipation & 12.7875 & 14.6524\\
Added microwave dissipation & 1.6419 & 0.9416\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
With mismatched profiles, the orthogonal pair dissipates $2.7759\%$, included in the atomic total.
Of the $35.0133$-percentage-point drop in conversion, $33.8487$ points appear as
increased reflection. Mode mismatch mainly increases reflection here; the conversion
penalty is neither $1-Q^2$ nor the orthogonal pair's dissipation.

\section{Validity and notebook conventions}
Anharmonic motion, resolved sidebands, inhomogeneous controls or detunings, and
additional independent optical channels require an extended model.
The notebooks and direct matrix solutions verify the six-mode reduction, $Q=1$
limit, benchmark, and flux conservation; they do not validate the geometry.

In \texttt{Abhishek\_Converter\_QPM.nb}, set $\epsilon=1$ and
$\gadd=\gamma_r$ (the latter is implicit). Its $\kappa,\kappa_\mu$ denote
$\kappa_o,\kappa_m$; the calculator's $G_{\rm opt},G_{\rm mm}$ denote $G,G_\mu$.
The notebook uses $x,z$ for the two axes called $x,y$ here.
Since \texttt{ConverterEfficQ} retains its forcing amplitude \texttt{Ein},
\begin{equation}
 S_{mo}=\sqrt{\eta_o\eta_m}\,
 \frac{\texttt{ConverterEfficQ}}{\texttt{Ein}},\qquad
 \eta=\eta_o\eta_m\,\texttt{NormalizedEfficiencyQ}.
 \label{eq:notebook-normalization}
\end{equation}
For real nonzero drive,
$\texttt{NormalizedEfficiencyQ}=\texttt{EfficiencyQ}/\texttt{Ein}^2$.
\texttt{EfficiencyQWithPorts} multiplies the unnormalized expression, so use
\texttt{Ein=1} or divide its result by $\texttt{Ein}^2$.
\texttt{SigmaPerp} and \texttt{OnResonanceEfficiencyQ} implement
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:sigma} and \eqref{eq:eta0}, the latter at unit ports.
Use \texttt{parmsScreenshot} for Eq.~\eqref{eq:benchmark}; \texttt{parmsOriginal}
retains the original parameters. Keep $Q$ symbolic until applying \texttt{qRule}.
